# The VA Loan Checklist for Wichita Area Buyers

*What the VA loan actually requires, what it does not, and where the
process tends to stall locally.*

---

## Your entitlement, explained once

Entitlement is the VA's guarantee to the lender, not a dollar amount
handed to you. It determines how much you can borrow with no down
payment.

- **Full entitlement** generally applies if you have never used the
  benefit, or you used it before and either paid the loan off and sold
  the property, or had a prior VA loan foreclosure paid in full.
- **Partial (or remaining) entitlement** applies if you used the benefit
  before and still own the property, or the prior use was not fully
  restored. It does not mean you cannot buy again. It changes the
  no-down-payment amount available on the next purchase.

The exact figure available to you is on your Certificate of Eligibility,
and your lender reads it directly rather than estimating it. This is
worth confirming before you fall in love with a specific price range.

## The Certificate of Eligibility

The Certificate of Eligibility, or COE, is the document that proves your
entitlement to a lender.

- Most lenders who close VA loans regularly can pull it for you directly
  through the VA's system, usually in minutes.
- You can also request it yourself through the VA's eBenefits portal or
  by mail, which takes longer.
- If it comes back showing less entitlement than you expected, or with an
  error, that is a lender conversation to have immediately, not something
  to work around later. It is the single item most likely to sit waiting
  on someone else, so requesting it before you are seriously house
  hunting takes it off the critical path entirely.

## What the VA appraisal looks for

A VA appraisal does two jobs at once: it checks the value, the same as
any appraisal, and it checks the property against the VA's Minimum
Property Requirements, or MPRs.

The MPRs are about the house being livable, structurally sound, and
sanitary.
That generally covers things like:

- A working, adequately sized heating system
- No exposed wiring or other electrical hazards
- A roof that keeps water out for the reasonably near future
- No evidence of wood-destroying insects or active water intrusion
- Water and sewage disposal that meets local code

## The funding fee, in dollars

The VA funding fee is a percentage of the loan amount, and it varies by:

- Whether this is your first use of the benefit or a subsequent use
- Your down payment amount, if any
- Whether it is a purchase or a refinance

## Timing a purchase around orders

When there is a report date on the other end, the schedule gets built
backward from it rather than forward from today.

1. **Start with the date you have to be there.** Every other deadline in
   the transaction gets worked back from this one, including the last day
   an offer can be accepted and still close in time.
2. **Get the Certificate of Eligibility early**, for the reason above.
   It is the item most likely to cause a delay if it is left for later.
3. **Get pre-approved with a lender who closes VA loans regularly.** Not
   every lender does volume in these, and the ones that do know what the
   appraisal will ask for, which shortens the back half of the
   transaction.
4. **Filter for houses that will pass the appraisal**, not just houses
   you like. Alex flags obvious MPR problems while you are still walking
   through a house, before an offer gets written on one that will not
   clear in your window.
5. **Write the offer with the timeline in it.** A seller weighing several
   offers treats a firm, explained closing date as lower risk, not
   higher, especially paired with a lender who has already confirmed
   where the loan stands.

## What a seller needs to hear about a VA offer

Some sellers hesitate on VA offers because of things they have heard
rather than things they have experienced on a specific transaction. The
plain answers:

- **"VA appraisals kill deals."** A VA appraisal checks condition as well
  as value. A house in ordinary, maintained condition clears it the same
  as any other appraisal would.
- **"VA buyers can't close on time."** Closing speed is a function of the
  lender and the file, not the loan program. A lender who closes VA loans
  regularly moves at the same pace as any other well-prepared file.
- **"The seller pays extra fees on a VA loan."** What a seller pays is
  negotiated the same way it is on any offer. The VA restricts certain
  fees from being charged to the buyer, which is not the same as
  requiring the seller to pay them, and this is a point worth having
  Alex walk through directly with a specific offer in hand rather than as
  a general rule.

Presenting a VA offer with the closing timeline and the lender's status
already attached answers most of this before the objection is raised at
all.
